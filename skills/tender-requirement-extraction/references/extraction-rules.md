# Extraction rules

## Source precedence

Record the precedence stated by the procurement documents. Do not invent a default legal order. When the source does not state precedence, retain the conflict and request clarification.

An amendment may add, replace, or remove a requirement. Preserve both source references and state the resulting relationship.

## Atomic rows

Create separate rows when a sentence contains obligations with different:

- Responsible teams.
- Product or service components.
- Evidence or certificate requirements.
- Delivery dates.
- Acceptance methods.
- Response or deviation outcomes.

Keep related context in notes so splitting does not change meaning.

## Categories

- Technical: functions, performance, architecture, interface, security, data, hardware, software, environment, drawings.
- Business: business process, user, service process, reporting, operating objective.
- Qualification: company, personnel, certification, performance record, authorization.
- Scoring: evaluated item, point value, evidence rule, scoring threshold.
- Schedule: milestone, delivery, installation, trial, support response time.
- Acceptance: test method, sample, threshold, FAT, SAT, sign-off, defect rule.
- Delivery: document, training, spare, drawing, source code, license, handover.
- Commercial: price, payment, bond, tax, legal or contract term. Extract and assign; do not draft unsupported responses.

## Risk markers

Capture the exact marker and wording for mandatory, star, rejection, disqualification, invalid bid, material deviation, or equivalent clauses. Do not rely on color or formatting alone when the extracted text does not preserve it.

## Normalization

Normalized text should state one testable obligation with its object, action or property, threshold, condition, timing, and acceptance evidence when available. Use "unknown" or a review note for missing elements rather than filling them in.
